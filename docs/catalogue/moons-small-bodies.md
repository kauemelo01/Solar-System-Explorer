# Moons of dwarf planets and asteroids

**10 moons:** 6 moons of dwarf planets and candidates, and 4 asteroid moons. Pluto's moons have [their own page](moons-pluto.md).

The *Surface image* column says what the app draws on each body; these moons are drawn as plain shapes. Details: [Surface maps](../sources.md#surface-maps). Where JPL Horizons has a satellite solution, the orbit is fitted to it; Hiʻiaka's and Namaka's are fitted to the ephemeris of Proudfoot et al. 2024; the others come from the peer-reviewed orbit paper listed. The *a*, *e*, *i* and *P* columns are the values used by the app. The JPL IDs are Horizons SPK IDs.

Moons whose orbit could not be found in a verifiable published source are not included (a [limitation of the model](../sources.md#limitations-of-the-model); see [What's not included](../sources.md#whats-not-included)); this is why Makemake's moon (S/2015 (136472) 1, "MK2", whose orbit is only bounded), Ida's moon Dactyl, Polymele's moon Shaun and Dinkinesh's moon Selam (whose plane and phase are not published: only its distance, 3.1 km, and period, 52.7 h, Levison et al. 2024) are absent. They are on the [Watchlist](../sources.md#watchlist).

## Notes

- **Fitted orbits.** Dysnomia, Weywot, Vanth and Dimorphos follow orbits fitted to their JPL Horizons satellite solutions, and Hiʻiaka and Namaka orbits fitted to the 2005–2035 ephemeris of Proudfoot et al. 2024 (a decade of Hubble and Keck observations, with Haumea's flattening and the two moons' pulls on each other). Checked with the app's own code on dates not used in the fits: Dysnomia, Weywot and Vanth within 0.004°, Dimorphos typically 1.1° (at most 6.9°), Hiʻiaka 0.6° (1.8°), Namaka 1.5° (7.5°), as seen from the primary. Outside the range their source covers the card says *Estimated position*. Before Release 4 Hiʻiaka and Namaka used the Ragozzine & Brown 2009 elements as printed in its Table 2, which put their orbits in the wrong plane and turning the wrong way (60° off on average), and the others used JPL's osculating elements for 2025-Jan-1, which drift (Vanth 40° off by 2000); Dimorphos had the osculating period, 11.79 h, instead of its real 11.36 h.
- **DART.** NASA's DART spacecraft hit Dimorphos on 26 September 2022 and shortened its orbit from 11.92 h to 11.36 h; the app switches between the two fitted orbits within an hour of the impact.
- **Queta and Skamandrios** keep their published orbits, which the app reproduces exactly at their epochs: no independent ephemeris agrees with them (IMCCE's Miriade service gives other orbits for both, which contradict these peer-reviewed solutions). Their published periods are known to ±0.06 days (Queta) and ±0.0003 days (Skamandrios), so by 2026 their positions along the orbit are uncertain by about ±8° and ±90°; Skamandrios's orbit also precesses (Marchis et al. 2014), which the app does not follow (a [limitation of the model](../sources.md#limitations-of-the-model)).
- **Frames.** Fitted orbits are J2000 ecliptic, relative to the primary. Queta's is J2000 ecliptic as published; Skamandrios's are J2000 equatorial as published, and its inclination is given in that frame (50.1° to Hektor's equator). Xiangliu's published orbit has a mirror ambiguity (prograde or retrograde); the app uses the prograde solution, with angles taken in the J2000 equatorial frame of the published astrometry, so its orientation is uncertain (a [limitation of the model](../sources.md#limitations-of-the-model)).
- **Sizes.** Dysnomia, Weywot and Vanth radii are from their JPL Horizons records. Hiʻiaka (≈ 320 km) and Namaka (≈ 160 km) diameters are from Ragozzine & Brown (2009) as compiled by Johnston's Archive. Dimorphos is 160 m across ([NASA DART](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dart/)). Xiangliu is smaller than 100 km (Kiss et al. 2019); the app uses a placeholder, marked *est.* (a [limitation of the model](../sources.md#limitations-of-the-model)). Skamandrios is 12 ± 3 km across (Marchis et al. 2014, from its brightness, assuming Hektor's albedo) and Queta 1.2 ± 0.4 km (Noll et al. 2020, from its brightness). Menoetius's radius (52 km) is from its JPL Horizons record
- **In the app.** The moons menu lists them under their parent: *Dwarf planets & candidates* (Eris, Haumea, then the candidates Quaoar, Orcus and Gonggong, after Pluto) and *Asteroids* (Didymos, Hektor, Eurybates, Patroclus), with All / None for each group. A moon is shown when it is switched on and its parent's group is shown (*Dwarf planets*, *Candidates* or *Asteroids & centaurs*); switching a moon on also switches its parent's group on.
