# Moons of Uranus

**29 moons.** Positions along the orbits are **fitted to JPL Horizons over 1800–2050** ([Moon orbits](../sources.md#moon-orbits)): checked against Horizons on other dates, the typical error for this planet's moons is 0.8° (median), and the least accurate is Desdemona at 2.9°. The *a*, *e*, *i* and *P* columns below are the **JPL mean orbital elements** (Laplace-plane or ecliptic, as published by JPL SSD); the fitted orbits keep these shapes, or refine them where Horizons shows they change. Every moon links to its **JPL Horizons** object record by NAIF ID, and to its NASA Science page where NASA has published one.

The *Surface image* column says what the app draws on each body: real photos, partial maps, painted illustrations or plain shapes. Details and sources: [Surface maps](../sources.md#surface-maps).

- NASA Science overview: <https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/>
- JPL mean elements table: <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/sep.html>
- JPL physical parameters: <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/>
- JPL discovery circumstances: <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/discovery.html>
- S. S. Sheppard (Carnegie Science), Uranus moons page: <https://sites.google.com/carnegiescience.edu/sheppard/moons/uranusmoons>
- IAU Minor Planet Center satellite announcements (MPECs): <https://minorplanetcenter.net/mpec/RecentMPECs.html>

Radius values marked *est.* are not published; the app uses a placeholder size for display only (a [limitation of the model](../sources.md#limitations-of-the-model)).
